MCC Docs / Trip Planning

Traveler Personas

Who Erik and Lisa are as travelers — inferred from the Chile/Patagonia planning. These are hypotheses for Erik to correct, not assertions.

Everything below is inferred from planning documents: lodge comparisons, dining research, itinerary choices, and decision patterns. Some of this will be wrong. The purpose is to give trip research a starting point so recommendations are relevant, not generic.

Food & Dining

Food is a primary travel motivation, not a supporting activity.

The Chile research included a standalone dining guide with restaurants categorized by tier (fine dining, upper mid-range, casual), meal-by-meal budget breakdowns, and specific restaurant recommendations with pricing. The dining budget ($1,200–1,800 for 2 people, non-hotel meals only) was treated as a first-class planning item.

Evidence: Dedicated chile-dining.html doc, Borago (Michelin star) on the must-do list, wine tour as a full-day activity.
Erik has an existing food reference frame.

Erik has eaten at Borago in Santiago and knows the Cerro Alegre dining scene in Valparaiso. He pushed back on the initial dining budget ($500–800) as too low, citing that it "would barely cover Santiago dinners alone." He engages with food as someone who has opinions, not just preferences.

Evidence: "Erik has been" badge on Borago, budget correction in dining doc.
Hypothesis

They want 1–2 memorable fine dining experiences per trip, supplemented by quality casual eating. Not every meal needs to be an event, but the food scene of a destination matters when choosing where to go.

Comfort & Luxury Level

Upper-tier comfort, not ultra-luxury.

The Chile lodges ranged from $5,700 to $20,000+ for 4 nights. They ruled out the budget options (Pampa Lodge, Kau) as "too budget for this trip" but also didn't default to the most expensive (Awasi at $19,500+, Tierra at $18,000+). Erik leaned toward Las Torres at ~$10,800 — the best value in the upper tier, not the most expensive option available.

Evidence: Ruled out budget lodges, leaned mid-premium (Las Torres), considered Ritz-Carlton Santiago at $350–450/night.
Value-aware despite high budget.

The lodge comparison doc carefully mapped price vs. feature tradeoffs (transfer time, included excursions, Wi-Fi, pool access). They didn't just pick the most expensive option. The $5,100 price difference between Las Torres and Rio Serrano was weighed against specific experience benefits (trailhead access, excursion variety), not dismissed.

Evidence: Detailed feature comparison table, Grey Glacier add-on cost noted as a differentiator ($274).
Hypothesis

They're comfortable spending $300–600/night on hotels and expect quality service, but they research and compare rather than defaulting to the highest price point. A $400/night boutique hotel that delivers a distinctive experience may be preferred over a $700/night generic luxury chain.

Activity Level & Pace

Moderate activity preferred over sustained intensity.

The Chile trip was deferred specifically because the core draw (Torres del Paine) requires 8–12 hour hikes at high difficulty. They built rest days and lighter alternatives into the itinerary (horseback riding, Cuernos viewpoint at 4 hours instead of full hike at 8 hours). They ruled out the puma tracking excursion ($1,300–2,200 pp) partly because Jan 4 was "better as a lighter day after two big hikes."

Evidence: Puma tracking ruled out for pace reasons (not just cost), trip deferred due to hiking intensity.
Walking and exploring, not trekking.

The non-Patagonia days in the Chile itinerary were: wine tasting, neighborhood walking in Valparaiso, Santiago city exploration, Cerro San Cristobal. These are urban/cultural activities with organic physical movement, not planned exercise.

Evidence: Chile itinerary days 1, 2, 3, 8, 9 were all cultural/food-focused.
Hypothesis

Ideal activity level: 2–4 hours of walking/exploring per day, with the option to do more on a given day. No multi-hour endurance activities. Day trips to nature are welcome if the main activity is scenic (driving, boat rides, short walks) rather than athletic.

Planning Style

Lisa drives initial research; Erik refines and executes.

Lisa did the original planning session that produced destinations, hotels, flights, excursions, and budget tiers. Erik then verified pricing on booking sites, narrowed lodge options with detailed comparisons, sent inquiry emails, and built the trip status documentation. The workflow is collaborative but with clear roles.

Evidence: "What's Been Done" section attributes 5 items to Lisa (research) and 6 to Erik (verification, comparison, emails).
Research-heavy before committing.

The Chile trip had zero bookings made despite thorough research on 8 lodges, 6+ restaurants, flight routes, wine tours, and a 9-day itinerary. They were still in the "booking" phase, with open questions routed to Lisa for decisions. This suggests they prefer to have all information before pulling the trigger, rather than booking incrementally.

Evidence: "Bookings Made: 0 of 8" despite research status being "Done."

Travel Logistics

Nonstop or minimal connections preferred.

The Chile plan specifically identified the United nonstop route via Houston as the decided flight option. When Atacama was considered, the extra internal flights were cited as "wasted travel days." They optimize for time at the destination, not in transit.

Evidence: "Fly United via Houston (IAH)" locked in early, Atacama dropped partly for transit overhead.
Hypothesis

For international trips, one connection is acceptable if unavoidable. For domestic trips (Palm Springs), direct flights are strongly preferred. Internal flights within a multi-city trip are fine if the destinations justify the time cost — the Atacama objection was about the destination being sold out, not the flight itself.

NYE Expectations

NYE is a planned event, not incidental.

The Chile plan placed NYE in Valparaiso specifically for the fireworks spectacle ("one of South America's biggest fireworks shows, right over the bay"). They chose a hotel with rooftop views. The dining guide asked Lisa to decide between booking a special restaurant vs. eating at the hotel rooftop. NYE is meant to be memorable.

Evidence: Valparaiso selected for NYE fireworks, Hotel Fauna chosen for rooftop view, NYE dinner treated as a decision point.
