Traveler Personas

Who Erik and Lisa are as travelers — direct input, not inferences.

Food & Dining

Dining is a primary travel motivation, driven by Lisa. The food scene of a destination matters when choosing where to go.

Lisa
Experienced, opinionated, restriction-aware

Extensive fine dining experience but does not crave Michelin stars — she often finds the long tasting menus tedious. Reads food blogs and guide books; appreciates well-referenced recommendations.

Restrictions: Allergic to dairy, does not eat red meat or gluten. Willing to compromise if the restaurant is genuinely good.

Lisa is Korean-American — her standards for Asian food are high. Generic "Asian fusion" won't cut it.

Erik
Adventurous, budget-conscious gourmand

Will try everything once. Enjoys exploring local food scenes and street food. Dislikes overpaying for a subpar meal — value matters more than prestige.

Together

Quality dining without chasing luxury for its own sake. 1–2 memorable meals per trip, supplemented by well-researched casual eating. Walking food tours are a favorite, especially in places with strong ethnic cuisine. A great lunch spot can bridge two walking activities (museums, city tours).

Activity Level & Fitness

Both go to the gym daily at home, but training is not a trip priority.

Lisa
Likes a good gym, especially in familiar destinations

Does Solidcore, Barry's, and similar. When traveling to places she knows (London, etc.), she'll seek out a good gym. A hotel with a quality gym is a plus. For new destinations, she's flexible about skipping workouts.

Erik
Active but doesn't seek out gyms on trips

Physical activity comes from the trip itself — walking, exploring, hiking.

Together

Both are physically capable but don't plan trips around exercise. The ideal day has two activities: breakfast, day activity, break, evening activity. A heavy brunch means a lighter or no lunch (or a snack between activities). They want room for relaxing at the hotel on some afternoons — planned but with reactive flexibility.

Weather & Comfort

Erik
Runs warm

Hot weather may require more breaks, shade, and hydration planning. Indoor-heavy itineraries work better in heat.

Lisa
Runs cold

Cold weather means more indoor activities and recharge breaks. Packing guidance matters — she wants to know what to expect so she can prepare.

Together

Weather is an important planning factor. Trip research should include climate data and packing notes. Shoulder seasons with moderate temperatures tend to work best for both.

Lodging

Lisa
Extensive travel experience, prefers American-style comfort

Calibrated expectations from many high-end hotels. Values comfort, cleanliness, reliable service. A good gym and quality bedding matter.

Erik
Budget-conscious, comfortable roughing it

Doesn't need luxury and actively dislikes conspicuous consumption and materialism. Would rather spend on experiences (food, activities) than a nicer room.

Together

They bias toward things that are nice, but not luxury for its own sake. A $400/night boutique hotel with character beats a $700/night generic luxury chain. Value-conscious: they research and compare rather than defaulting to the highest price point.

Interests & Culture

Art, museums, and shows

Both love art. Museums are a go-to activity and work well as walking-based daytime plans. Erik enjoys reading and gravitates toward intellectual and literary sites — a museum of Nobel laureates, for example, would be a draw.

Opera & classical music

Erik is passionate about opera and will always make time for a performance in a new city. Choral music is closely related — a cathedral choir or choral concert is equally compelling. When researching a destination, check the local opera house and concert schedule for the travel dates.

Walking food tours

A recurring favorite, especially in cities with strong ethnic cuisine. Combines food discovery with neighborhood exploration.

Planning Style

Lisa
Prefers a clear plan, research-driven

Wants to know what's happening and when. Reads food blogs, guide books, and published reviews. A well-referenced recommendation carries weight. Having things mapped out reduces stress and lets her enjoy the trip.

Erik
More willing to go with the flow

Comfortable with open blocks and spontaneous decisions. Adapts on the fly. Appreciates Lisa's research and benefits from the structure.

Together

The ideal plan has clear structure with built-in flexibility. Key meals and activities are booked or identified in advance, but some afternoons and evenings are left open. "Planned but reactive." Lisa drives initial research; Erik refines and executes logistics. Plans revolve around activity and dining options.

Travel Logistics

Nonstop or minimal connections preferred.

Optimize for time at the destination. One connection is acceptable for international trips if unavoidable. Direct flights strongly preferred for domestic.

NYE

NYE is a planned event, not incidental.

They want to plan for it specifically — a memorable setting, good dinner, and a sense of occasion. Doesn't have to be extravagant, but should be intentional.
