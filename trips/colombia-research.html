Why Colombia

Based on the traveler personas: Colombia hits every preference — world-class food scene (Leo, El Cielo, Celele), cultural depth, walkable historic cities, warm weather, and moderate activity level. The pace is naturally relaxed: you explore neighborhoods, eat well, and absorb culture. No endurance activities required. And Cartagena's NYE — fireworks over the Caribbean, champagne at midnight in a colonial fortress — is as memorable as Valparaiso.

Recommended Route

The route is designed around the NYE anchor: Cartagena for New Year's Eve, with Bogota on the front end (culture and food) and Medellin on the back end (spring climate, cafes, street art).

Dates City Nights Why
Dec 29–31 Bogota 2 Arrive, decompress, food scene, culture
Dec 31–Jan 3 Cartagena 3 NYE on the Caribbean, old city, beaches, food
Jan 3–5 Medellin 2 Spring climate, neighborhoods, cafes, Guatape day trip option
Jan 5–6 Fly home — MDE → IAH (connecting via BOG or direct on United/Avianca)

Alternative: Swap the order to Bogota → Medellin → Cartagena, ending at the beach. This puts Cartagena last (more relaxed ending) but means NYE is in Medellin instead. Cartagena has the stronger NYE scene.

Restaurants Food capital

Bogota's Zona G (the "G" stands for "gastronomico") is one of Latin America's densest fine dining corridors.

Leo Cocina y Cava (LEO)
Chef Leonor Espinosa. Latin America's 50 Best. Colombian ingredients from every region.
~$300–450 for 2 (tasting menu + pairing)
Signature ingredients include big-butted ants from Santander and fermented yuca from the Amazon. The defining Colombian fine dining experience. Book weeks ahead. Website
El Cielo
Chef Juan Manuel Barrientos (first Colombian Michelin star, for the Miami location). 12-course tasting menu.
~$250–350 for 2 (tasting menu + pairing)
Theatrical, multi-sensory dining. Wine pairings. elcielorestaurant.com
Harry Sasson
106-year-old red-brick mansion. Live-fire cooking. Steak, seafood, robata.
~$150–250 for 2
The classic power-dining spot in Bogota. Less avant-garde than Leo/El Cielo but consistently excellent. Reservations essential.
Villanos en Bermudas
Young chefs (Mexican + Argentine). Colombia's most ambitious restaurant.
~$200–300 for 2
Small, creative, hard to get a table. If the vibe is right, this could be the highlight meal of the trip.

Activities (Bogota)

  • Usaquen Flea Market (Sundays) — artisan goods, street food, live music. Walkable from W Bogota.
  • La Candelaria — historic center, Botero Museum (free), Gold Museum. Street art, colonial architecture. 2–3 hour walking exploration.
  • Monserrate — cable car/funicular to 10,341 ft viewpoint over the city. Restaurant at the top. ~1–2 hours.
  • Paloquemao Market — massive covered market. Tropical fruits, flowers, local food stalls. Best in the morning.
  • Coffee tasting — specialty cafes in Chapinero serve single-origin Colombian coffee. Azahar, Libertario, Amor Perfecto.

NYE in Cartagena

Cartagena is Colombia's #1 NYE destination. The walled city (Ciudad Amurallada) and Getsemani run parallel programs: hotel galas, street parties, rooftop celebrations, and fireworks over the bay.

Baluarte San Francisco Javier — NYE Gala
Historic fortress. Welcome drink, wine dinner, champagne toast at midnight, 12 grapes tradition, fireworks.
Needs verification — likely $150–300/person
Spectacular setting: a colonial-era fortress on the water. Fireworks display from the venue. Eventbrite listing

The Colombian NYE tradition: 12 grapes at midnight (one for each month wish), fireworks, dancing in the streets. Walk out of your hotel into the party.

Activities (Cartagena)

  • Walled City walking — just wander. Colonial architecture, street art, plazas, churches. 2–4 hours of organic exploring.
  • Getsemani neighborhood — the hip neighborhood outside the walls. Street art, cafes, nightlife. Walk from the walled city.
  • Rosario Islands day trip — boat to the archipelago (~1 hour). Snorkeling, beaches, seafood lunch. Full-day excursion. The "easy nature" option.
  • Castillo San Felipe — the massive hilltop fortress. 1–2 hour visit. Good sunset views.
  • Sunset cocktails at Cafe del Mar — on the city walls, overlooking the ocean. The classic Cartagena evening activity.

Activities (Medellin)

  • Comuna 13 — guided walking tour of the neighborhood transformed by escalators and street art. 2–3 hours. The defining Medellin experience.
  • El Poblado / Provenza — walk the tree-lined streets, cafes, boutiques. The Laureles neighborhood is the more local alternative.
  • Guatape day trip — colorful lakeside town, 2 hours from Medellin. Climb the 740-step Piedra del Penol for the view. Full day excursion. Worth it if the energy is there.
  • Botanical Garden — free, peaceful, beautiful. Good morning activity.
  • Coffee farm tour — day trips to the Coffee Region (Eje Cafetero) are possible from Medellin but long (3+ hours each way). Better as a focused trip another time.
  • Metro cable — ride the city's cable car system for valley views. Connects to Parque Arvi nature reserve at the top. 2–3 hours.

Weather

City High Low Conditions
Bogota (Dec 29–31) 66°F (19°C) 45°F (7°C) Dry season. Cool, clear skies. Pack layers — altitude makes evenings chilly.
Cartagena (Dec 31–Jan 3) 88°F (31°C) 77°F (25°C) Hot, sunny, dry. Humidity high but no rain. Light clothing, sunscreen.
Medellin (Jan 3–5) 82°F (28°C) 63°F (17°C) Warm days, mild evenings. Comfortable year-round. Occasional brief afternoon shower.

December–January is dry season across most of Colombia. This is peak tourist season but also the best weather window. All three cities are pleasant — the temperature swing between Bogota (cool) and Cartagena (tropical) is dramatic, similar to the Santiago-to-Patagonia shift in the Chile plan.

Safety

The U.S. State Department rates Colombia at Level 3 ("Reconsider Travel") due to crime and civil unrest in specific regions. However, the three tourist cities are well-established and safe with standard precautions:

  • Bogota: Tourist districts (Chapinero, Zona Rosa, Usaquen, Zona G) are well-policed and walkable. Use registered taxis or Uber (widely available).
  • Cartagena: The walled city and Getsemani are well-patrolled, lively, and safe into the night. The main risk is petty theft (pickpockets in crowded areas).
  • Medellin: El Poblado and Laureles are the visitor zones — safe, walkable, full of restaurants and cafes. Use Uber or registered transport.

Practical: use Uber (legal and ubiquitous), don't flash expensive jewelry, stay in tourist neighborhoods at night. These are the same precautions you'd take in any Latin American city. The Level 3 advisory reflects specific rural areas, not Bogota/Cartagena/Medellin.

Visa & Logistics

  • Visa: US passport holders get 90-day visa-free entry. Stamped at immigration on arrival.
  • Currency: Colombian Peso (COP). ~4,100 COP = $1 USD (as of late 2026). Credit cards widely accepted in tourist areas. ATMs available.
  • Language: Spanish. Tourist areas in all three cities have English-speaking staff at hotels and upscale restaurants. Outside of tourist zones, basic Spanish is very helpful.
  • Altitude: Bogota is at 8,660 ft. Take it easy the first day — altitude can cause mild headache and fatigue. Cartagena and Medellin are at lower elevations with no altitude concern.

Suggested Itinerary

Dec 29 (Mon) — Arrive Bogota
Morning flight IAH → BOG (direct, ~5h). Check into Four Seasons Casa Medina. Slow afternoon — acclimate to altitude. Walk Zona G. Dinner at Harry Sasson.
Dec 30 (Tue) — Bogota Exploring
Morning: Paloquemao Market (tropical fruits, flowers, local food). Midday: La Candelaria walking — Botero Museum, Gold Museum, street art. Afternoon: Monserrate cable car for city views. Dinner: LEO (tasting menu, the highlight meal).
Dec 31 (Wed) — Fly to Cartagena + NYE
Morning flight BOG → CTG (~1h 40m). Check into Sofitel Legend Santa Clara or Casa San Agustin. Afternoon: walk the walled city, Getsemani. Evening: NYE dinner at the hotel gala or Baluarte San Francisco Javier fortress. Midnight: fireworks over the Caribbean, 12 grapes tradition, champagne in the streets.
Jan 1 (Thu) — Cartagena Recovery + Exploration
Late start. Brunch in Getsemani. Afternoon: Castillo San Felipe or poolside at the hotel. Sunset cocktails at Cafe del Mar on the city walls. Dinner: Celele (tasting menu).
Jan 2 (Fri) — Rosario Islands Day Trip
Full-day boat trip to Rosario Islands (~1h each way). Snorkeling, beach time, seafood lunch on the island. Return to Cartagena for a light dinner. Or: skip the islands and do a second day exploring the old city + ceviche at La Cevicheria.
Jan 3 (Sat) — Fly to Medellin
Morning flight CTG → MDE (~1h 15m). Check into The Charlee. Afternoon: walk El Poblado / Provenza. Coffee at Pergamino or Hija Mia. Dinner: Carmen.
Jan 4 (Sun) — Medellin
Morning: guided tour of Comuna 13 (street art, escalators, music). Afternoon: Botanical Garden or metro cable to Parque Arvi. Evening: casual dinner, Mondongo's or street food. Or: full-day Guatape trip (colorful town, Piedra del Penol rock, lake views — more active).
Jan 5 (Mon) — Fly Home
Morning coffee in Laureles. MDE → IAH (connecting via BOG, or direct if available). Arrive Houston evening.

Pacing note: This itinerary has 1–2 activities per day with generous free time. No multi-hour hikes. The longest excursion is the Rosario Islands boat trip, which is beach relaxation with a scenic ride. Guatape is the most active option (740 steps to the viewpoint) and is listed as an alternative, not a default.

Why This Could Work

  • Food scene is world-class. LEO, El Cielo, Celele, Harry Sasson, Carmen — three cities with distinct culinary identities. Bogota has avant-garde Colombian, Cartagena has Caribbean seafood fusion, Medellin has regional Antioqueno. This matches the food-forward travel style.
  • Right activity level. Walking neighborhoods, visiting museums, taking cable cars, sitting in plazas. Everything is optional and low-intensity. The only "active" option (Guatape) is listed as an alternative.
  • NYE in Cartagena is memorable. Fireworks over the Caribbean, colonial fortress galas, 12 grapes at midnight, dancing in the streets. This rivals the Valparaiso NYE they were planning.
  • Direct flights from Houston. IAH → BOG is 5 hours nonstop on United or Avianca. Same airline, same airport as the Chile plan.
  • Better value. Premium tier here ($8.5–10.5k) costs less than Chile's budget tier ($12–13k). The dollars go further without sacrificing quality.
  • Cultural depth. Three distinct cities with different histories, climates, and characters. More variety than Chile's Santiago+Patagonia split.
  • No endurance activities. Nothing in this itinerary requires sustained physical effort. The Chile trip was deferred for hiking intensity — that concern does not apply here.

Tradeoffs

  • Three cities = more logistics. Two internal flights, three hotel check-ins. More moving parts than a single-destination trip. This is the same number of cities as Chile (Santiago, Valparaiso, Patagonia) but with air travel between all three.
  • Safety perception. Colombia's Level 3 advisory can be unsettling even when the tourist cities are safe. Some people are more comfortable with this than others.
  • Language. Spanish is essential outside tourist zones. Hotels and upscale restaurants speak English, but taxis, markets, and casual spots may not. This was also true for Chile.
  • Peak season. December/January is high season. Hotels and flights will be at peak pricing. Book early — especially Cartagena hotels for NYE.
  • Bogota altitude. At 8,660 ft, the first day may involve mild altitude effects (headache, fatigue). Not a dealbreaker, but worth noting. Drink water, take it easy.
