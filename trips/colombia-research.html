Why Colombia (Updated)

Persona fit

Caribbean seafood is naturally dairy-free and often gluten-free (ceviche, grilled fish, arepas). Walking food tours in Getsemani hit the ethnic-cuisine-on-foot pattern both prefer. Cartagena's walled city is museums + walking + plazas at exactly the right pace. Medellin adds art, spring climate, and neighborhood cafes. No endurance activities. Two things per day with breaks.

  • Food scene aligns with both profiles. Erik gets adventurous street food and value; Lisa gets well-researched, accommodation-friendly fine dining. Caribbean cuisine is naturally friendly to her restrictions.
  • Weather is favorable under El Nino. Unlike California (wetter), El Nino makes Colombia drier and warmer. IDEAM forecasts below-average precipitation and above-average temperatures Dec 2026–Mar 2027. Dry season with extra dry.
  • Intimate NYE, not a party. Cartagena has options ranging from fortress galas to quiet rooftop dinners to walking out into the plaza at midnight. You pick the vibe.
  • Good art and culture. Museum of Antioquia (Botero), Museo de Arte Moderno, street art everywhere, colonial architecture. Museums as daytime activity fits the two-per-day pattern.
  • Direct flights from Houston. IAH → CTG connections via BOG are frequent on United and Avianca (~6h total). Open-jaw return MDE → IAH.

The Colombian tradition: 12 grapes at midnight (one wish per month). Walk out of your hotel, join the celebration for 20 minutes, walk back. No commitment, no ticket. Can combine with an earlier dinner reservation.
NYE recommendation

Walking Food Tour

Cartagena Walled City Food Tour
2–3 hours walking through the old city and Getsemani. 8–10 tastings. Afro-Indigenous-European fusion cuisine history.
~$50–80/person
This is the walking-food-tour-with-ethnic-cuisine pattern both love. Covers ceviche, arepas, tropical fruit, coconut-based dishes, and the African/Spanish/indigenous culinary story of the Caribbean coast. Evening tours (5–9pm) are cooler and catch vendors at peak. GetYourGuide · Viator (private)

Activities (Cartagena)

  • Walled City walking — just wander. Colonial architecture, street art, plazas, churches. 2–4 hours of organic exploring. Good morning activity before the heat.
  • Getsemani neighborhood — the hip neighborhood outside the walls. Street art, cafes. Walk from the walled city.
  • Castillo San Felipe — the massive hilltop fortress. 1–2 hour visit. Good sunset views. Erik note: afternoon heat + hill climb = bring water.
  • Rosario Islands day trip — boat to the archipelago (~1h). Snorkeling, beaches, seafood lunch. Full-day. The "easy nature" option for a day when you want less walking.
  • Sunset cocktails at Cafe del Mar — on the city walls, overlooking the ocean. The classic Cartagena evening ritual.
  • Hotel afternoon — pool, rest, recharge. Plan at least one of the four afternoons for this. Lisa note: the AC break is welcome after morning exploring in the heat.

Activities (Medellin)

  • Museum of Antioquia — Botero collection (paintings + sculptures), plus colonial and contemporary Colombian art. Plaza Botero outside has 23 bronze sculptures. 2–3 hours. Both: art museum + plaza walking. The kind of morning activity that works perfectly.
  • Museo de Arte Moderno — modern and contemporary art from Colombian and international artists. Smaller, focused. 1–2 hours.
  • Museo Casa de la Memoria — reflects on Colombia's history of violence. Moving and important. Erik: the kind of heavy, meaningful museum experience you gravitate toward.
  • Comuna 13 — guided walking tour. Street art, escalators, music, neighborhood transformation story. 2–3 hours. The defining Medellin experience.
  • El Poblado / Provenza — walk the tree-lined streets, cafes, boutiques. Good for a slow afternoon.
  • Metro cable — ride the city's cable car for valley views. Connects to Parque Arvi nature reserve. 2–3 hours.
  • Botanical Garden — free, peaceful, beautiful. Good morning activity.
  • Coffee farm day trip — day trips to coffee fincas outside Medellin (Jardin, ~3h, or closer options in Santa Elena). See the full bean-to-cup process, taste single-origin varieties. Erik: big coffee drinker, this is a highlight. Can combine with a scenic drive and lunch in a small town.
  • Guatape day trip (optional) — colorful lakeside town, 2 hours out. 740 steps to the Piedra del Penol viewpoint. Full day. Only if the energy is there — this is the most active option.

Weather & El Nino

El Nino favors Colombia. Unlike California (where Super El Nino brings record rain), El Nino makes Colombia drier and warmer. IDEAM forecasts below-average precipitation Dec 2026–Mar 2027. This is dry season with extra dry. No rain risk for this trip.

Safety

U.S. State Department: Level 3 ("Reconsider Travel") due to specific rural regions. The tourist cities are well-established and safe with standard precautions:

  • Cartagena: Walled city and Getsemani are well-patrolled, lively, safe into the night. Main risk is petty theft (pickpockets in crowds).
  • Medellin: El Poblado and Laureles are the visitor zones — safe, walkable, full of restaurants and cafes.

Use Uber (legal, ubiquitous), don't flash jewelry, stay in tourist neighborhoods at night. Same precautions as any Latin American city.

Visa & Logistics

  • Visa: US passport holders get 90-day visa-free entry.
  • Currency: Colombian Peso (COP). ~4,100 COP = $1 USD. Credit cards widely accepted in tourist areas.
  • Language: Spanish. Hotels and upscale restaurants speak English. Basic Spanish helpful elsewhere.
  • No altitude concern: Without Bogota, neither city has altitude issues. Cartagena is sea level, Medellin is at 4,900 ft (mild).

Suggested Itinerary

Dec 30 (Tue) — Arrive Cartagena
Morning flight IAH → CTG (~6h with connection). Check into Casa San Agustin. Slow afternoon — walk the walled city, get oriented. Sunset cocktails at Cafe del Mar. Casual dinner: La Cevicheria or Pezetarian.
Dec 31 (Wed) — NYE
Morning: walking food tour of Getsemani (2–3h, Afro-Indigenous-Caribbean flavors). Afternoon: hotel pool, rest, get ready. Evening: dinner at Celele or Marea (book 8:30pm). Walk to Plaza Santo Domingo for midnight — 12 grapes, fireworks over the bay, champagne in the streets. Walk back to hotel.
Jan 1 (Thu) — Recovery + Exploration
Late start. Brunch in the walled city. Afternoon: Castillo San Felipe (fortress, views, 1–2h) or just pool + hotel. Light evening: walk Getsemani, street food, early bed.
Jan 2 (Fri) — Rosario Islands or Second City Day
Option A: Full-day boat to Rosario Islands. Snorkeling, beach, seafood lunch on the island. Relaxed, nature-forward.
Option B: Second day exploring the old city. Morning museum or church tour, lunch at Mar y Zielo, afternoon walk. Sunset at Cafe del Mar again (it's worth repeating).
Jan 3 (Sat) — Fly to Medellin
Morning flight CTG → MDE (~1h 15m). Check into The Charlee. Afternoon: walk El Poblado / Provenza. Coffee at Pergamino. Dinner: Carmen.
Jan 4 (Sun) — Medellin
Morning: Museum of Antioquia + Plaza Botero (Botero sculptures). Lunch near the museum. Afternoon: guided tour of Comuna 13 (street art, escalators, 2–3h). Or: swap Comuna 13 for Botanical Garden + metro cable to Parque Arvi. Evening: Mondongo's or Sambombi for casual dinner.
Jan 5 (Mon) — Fly Home
Morning coffee in Laureles neighborhood. MDE → IAH (connecting via BOG). Arrive Houston evening.
Pacing check

Every day has at most two planned activities. Jan 1 is explicitly a recovery day. Jan 2 has a relaxed option (islands/beach) and a walkable option. No multi-hour endurance anything. Heavy meals anchor the schedule — light lunch after a food tour morning, real dinner in the evening.

Tradeoffs

  • One fewer city = less variety. Dropping Bogota means missing LEO, El Cielo, the Gold Museum, and Monserrate. But Lisa's been to Bogota — the novelty is lower. And two cities is a better pace for a couples trip.
  • Cartagena is touristy at NYE. Peak season, peak pricing, peak crowds in the walled city. Book hotels and restaurants early. The tradeoff: you get a naturally festive atmosphere without having to seek it out.
  • Safety perception. Colombia's Level 3 advisory can be unsettling even when tourist cities are safe.
  • Language. Spanish essential outside tourist zones. Hotels and upscale restaurants speak English.
  • Heat in Cartagena. 88°F with humidity. Erik will want shade and water breaks. Indoor activities during peak heat (1–3pm).

Bogota Deprioritized

Removed from the main route. Lisa has been — describes it as pretty but a "party scene." Neither is looking for that on this trip. If you want to add Bogota back as a 1-night stopover (arrive late, leave early for Cartagena), the food scene justifies a dinner at LEO or Villanos en Bermudas. But it's not necessary.
