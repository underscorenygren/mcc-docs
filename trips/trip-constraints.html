Hard Constraints

  • No visa required. Chile, Colombia, and Palm Springs (domestic) all meet this. US passport holders get 90-day visa-free entry to Colombia.
  • Direct or one-stop flights from Houston. Chile required a nonstop United route. Palm Springs has direct flights. Colombia (Bogota) has direct United and Avianca flights from IAH (~5 hours).
  • Moderate activity level. The Chile trip was deferred partly because the Patagonia hiking was too intensive. They want experiences, not endurance tests. Light walks, cultural exploration, and food are preferred over multi-hour high-difficulty hikes.

Soft Preferences (Inferred)

  • Food matters. The Chile research included a detailed dining guide with Michelin-star restaurants, wine tours, and meal-by-meal budgeting. Dining is a priority, not an afterthought.
  • Comfort/luxury lodging. They were comparing $5,700–$20,000 all-inclusive lodges in Patagonia and $350–500/night city hotels (Ritz-Carlton Santiago, boutique hotels in Valparaiso). This is upper-tier accommodation.
  • Cultural experiences over pure adventure. The Chile itinerary included wine country, street art in Valparaiso, Santiago food scene — not just outdoor activities.
  • Relaxed pace. The Chile itinerary had rest days built in and lighter alternatives for active days. No packed schedules.
  • NYE should be special. The Chile plan specifically centered NYE in Valparaiso for the fireworks spectacle. Whatever destination is chosen, NYE should be a highlight event.

What Changed

The Chile/Patagonia trip was deferred because the core draw (Torres del Paine) requires sustained high-intensity hiking that doesn't match the trip style Erik and Lisa want. The research was thorough — lodges, dining, flights, budget all mapped out — but the activity level was the dealbreaker.

The two alternatives being researched are:

  • Palm Springs — shorter NYE trip (3–4 days), domestic, low-effort luxury.
  • Colombia — same dates (Dec 29 – Jan 6), multi-city cultural trip with food focus.
